How to Use This Neapolitan Pizza Dough Ratio Calculator

Consistent pizza starts on a digital kitchen scale. Once you work in baker's percentages instead of cups, scaling from two Friday night pies to a twelve-pie backyard party takes ten seconds.

1. Match Hydration to Your Oven

High-heat pizza ovens like an Ooni or Roccbox bake a 250g pie in 60 to 90 seconds, so moisture barely evaporates. Keeping hydration at 60%–65% prevents a gummy center and keeps the dough easy to launch.

In a standard American home oven capped at 500°F–550°F, a bake takes 5 to 7 minutes. Raising hydration to 65%–68% offsets that longer bake time and keeps the crumb light on a preheated baking steel.

2. Pick the Right Flour Strength

Not every flour drinks water the same way. Standard all-purpose flour (10.5%–11.5% protein) starts feeling soupy above 65%. Italian Tipo 00 milled for long ferments (like Caputo Pizzeria or Red) handles 60%–65% cleanly.

If you push past 68% hydration for Detroit pans or airy home-oven pies, switch to a 12.7%+ high-protein bread flour. The extra gluten builds structure so the dough holds gas instead of slumping flat.

3. Plan Your Cold Fermentation

The default 0.2% instant dry yeast is tuned for a 24- to 48-hour cold ferment in a 38°F–40°F refrigerator. Long, slow fermentation relaxes gluten for effortless hand-stretching and builds complex flavor.

Making same-day dough at room temperature (70°F–72°F)? Bump instant yeast up to 0.4%–0.6%, bulk ferment for 2 hours, ball the dough, and give it 4 to 5 hours in a covered proofing box before stretching.

Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers to the dough ratio, flour, and yeast questions home pizza makers ask most.

What is the best hydration percentage for Neapolitan pizza dough?

Traditional Neapolitan pizza dough sits between 60% and 65% hydration. If you are baking in an 850°F to 900°F wood-fired oven, Ooni, or Roccbox with Italian Tipo 00 flour, 62% to 65% gives you an airy, blistered rim without turning sticky on the peel. For a 500°F home oven, bump hydration to 65%–68% so the crust stays tender during a longer bake.

How does a pizza dough hydration calculator use baker's percentages?

In baker's math, total flour weight is always 100%, and every other ingredient is expressed as a percentage of that flour weight. For example, 65% hydration means 65 grams of water for every 100 grams of flour. To hit an exact target dough weight—like four 250g balls totaling 1,000g—the calculator divides total dough weight by the sum of all percentages.

Should I use Tipo 00 flour or high-protein bread flour in a home oven?

Use high-protein bread flour (around 12.5% to 13% protein, like King Arthur Bread Flour) in a standard home oven that tops out at 500°F–550°F. Unmalted Italian Tipo 00 flour is milled for 850°F+ decks and stays pale and cracker-like in a home oven unless you add diastatic malt or a little sugar. Save pure Tipo 00 for high-heat outdoor pizza ovens.

How do I convert between instant dry, active dry, and fresh yeast?

Use the standard 1 : 1.25 : 3 ratio by weight. If a recipe calls for 1 gram of instant dry yeast (IDY), swap in 1.25 grams of active dry yeast (ADY) or 3 grams of fresh cake yeast. Selecting a different yeast type in the calculator above adjusts the percentage automatically so your fermentation timing stays consistent.

Why does this neapolitan pizza dough ratio calculator leave out oil and sugar by default?

True Neapolitan dough uses only four ingredients: flour, water, salt, and yeast. At 850°F–900°F, added sugar scorches before the center bakes through and olive oil softens the crisp char. When you switch to the New York or Detroit preset—or turn them on in Custom mode—oil and sugar come back in to help browning and tenderness at 500°F–550°F.
